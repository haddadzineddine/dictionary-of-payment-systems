---
description: The legal entity that sells to the buyer in a card payment and takes responsibility for that sale and its disputes.
---

A merchant of record (MoR) is the legal entity that sells goods or services to the buyer in a transaction. Its name appears on the [cardholder's](./Cardholder.md) statement, it takes the payment, and it handles [refunds](./Refund.md) and [chargebacks](./Chargeback.md) for that sale. A software company may sell through an MoR, which buys or licenses the product and resells it to the end customer.

The term is widely used but is not defined identically everywhere. Visa's risk guidance uses it for a marketplace that contracts with the [acquirer](./Acquirer.md), rather than the underlying retailers. Visa's rules also look at who sells to the cardholder, whose name identifies the outlet, and who gives the cardholder recourse.

| Model                                             | Legal seller to the buyer | Who handles disputes              |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Direct merchant                                   | The business              | The business                      |
| [Payment facilitator](./Payment%20facilitator.md) | The sponsored merchant    | Sponsored merchant, via facilitator |
| Marketplace                                       | Usually the marketplace   | Marketplace, under its terms      |
| Merchant of record                                | The MoR                   | The MoR                           |

A facilitator helps a business accept cards while that business stays the seller. An MoR takes the seller's position, so it commonly also handles customer invoices and sales tax or VAT collection. The business then receives a net [payout](./Payout.md) from the MoR after its fee, taxes and refunds. Which obligations move, and in which countries, depends on the contract and local tax law.

The symptom customers notice is an unfamiliar name. Someone buys a tool from a small software company and sees the MoR's name on their statement, assumes it is fraud, and calls their bank. A clear [statement descriptor](./Statement%20descriptor.md) and a receipt naming both companies reduce these disputes.

_Usage:_

"Customers keep asking why their receipt shows a company they've never heard of."

"That's our merchant of record. Let's put our product name in the descriptor and explain the reseller on the receipt."
