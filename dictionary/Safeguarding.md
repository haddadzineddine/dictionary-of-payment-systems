---
description: Rules requiring non-bank payment and e-money firms to protect customer funds so they can be returned if the firm fails.
---

Safeguarding is the legal requirement for non-bank payment firms and e-money issuers to protect customer money so that it can be returned if the firm becomes insolvent. In the EU, the requirement comes from [PSD2](./PSD2.md) and the Electronic Money Directive. In the UK, it comes from the Payment Services Regulations 2017 and Electronic Money Regulations 2011, supplemented by Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) rules.

Firms typically safeguard in one of two ways:

| Method      | How it works                                                       |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Segregation | Keep relevant funds apart from the firm's own money, in a designated account or approved secure assets |
| Insurance or guarantee | A policy or guarantee pays out an equivalent amount if the firm fails |

Safeguarding is often confused with deposit insurance. A bank deposit may be covered by a national scheme, such as the UK's Financial Services Compensation Scheme. Money held with a payment or e-money firm generally is not. If the firm fails, customers rely on the safeguarded pool, and the insolvency process can cause delay or shortfall. A [rolling reserve](./Rolling%20reserve.md) is also different, because it protects a provider from a [merchant](./Merchant.md)'s losses.

The FCA's Policy Statement PS25/12 introduced a supplementary safeguarding regime that took effect on 7 May 2026, adding requirements such as monthly regulatory returns and, for many firms, annual safeguarding audits. US money transmitters are instead generally subject to state rules on permissible investments. Confirm the rules that apply to your licence with a qualified adviser.

The firm must know how much it owes customers at any time and prove that the safeguarded balance covers it, which requires an accurate [ledger](./Ledger.md) and regular [payment reconciliation](./Payment%20reconciliation.md) against the safeguarding account. Failures include customer funds sitting in an operating account during a [payout](./Payout.md) delay and shortfalls discovered days late. Alert on any difference.

_Usage:_

"Can we pay this supplier from the customer funds account until Friday?"

"No, that account is for safeguarding. Customer money can't fund operations, even temporarily."
